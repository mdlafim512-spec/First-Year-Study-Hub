1. What is a Nucleotide?

Nucleotides are the fundamental monomeric units that build nucleic acids (DNA and RNA). Beyond genetic storage, they serve as crucial components of coenzymes (like FAD and NAD+), play roles in metabolic regulation, and drive cellular energy transfers via ATP (the energy currency of the cell).

Nucleoside = Base + Sugar
Nucleotide = Base + Sugar + Phosphate

2. Purines and Pyrimidines

The nitrogenous bases found in nucleotides are aromatic heterocyclic compounds. They are structurally classified into two major families:

Feature Purines Pyrimidines
Structure Double-ring system Single-ring system
Numbering Direction Anticlockwise direction Clockwise direction
Bases Present Adenine (A), Guanine (G) Cytosine (C), Thymine (T), Uracil (U)

3. Major Bases in Nucleic Acids

While DNA and RNA share the same purine bases, they differ significantly in their choice of pyrimidine bases. DNA contains Thymine (T), whereas RNA contains Uracil (U). Thymine differs from Uracil simply by the presence of a methyl (-CH3) group.
